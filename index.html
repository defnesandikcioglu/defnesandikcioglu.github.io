Research

Auctions vs. Posted Prices: When Do Sellers Switch Formats?

Job Market Paper [Draft]

Sellers often do not know the demand for their good, and the selling mechanism itself can generate that information: an auction reveals demand but risks no sale and adds delay, while posting a price is immediate and certain but ends learning. This paper studies this trade-off on a wholesale used-car platform where sellers auction the same car repeatedly and may irreversibly switch to a posted price. I develop and estimate a dynamic structural model in which risk-averse sellers learn about demand from auction outcomes and weigh further experimentation against delay costs, using maximum likelihood computed with an EM algorithm and a machine-learning first stage. Auctions are precise experiments: a single auction resolves most of a seller's uncertainty, learning delivers about four-fifths of the value of full information, and bad news triggers prompt switching. Counterfactuals show the two formats are complements; mandating a switch after two auctions shortens time-on-market at no cost to sellers; and a reserve-recommendation tool and a standing guaranteed-purchase offer each raise seller welfare, the latter adopted precisely after sellers learn that demand is weak.

Simultaneous Auctions with Common and Private Value Bidders: Evidence from Online Car Auctions

Revise and Resubmit at The RAND Journal of Economics | 2026 [Draft]

Presented at NASMES 2026, SEA 2026 (scheduled)

This paper develops a novel structural model of second-price auctions with two types of bidders, motivated by data from an online used-car auction platform. Dealers, who have incentives to resell vehicles, have common value for the object and participate in multiple simultaneous auctions, while individual buyers have independent private values for the auctioned objects and submit only one bid. The model explicitly captures the interaction between common value and private value bidders and incorporates complementarities arising from multiple wins by dealers. I establish the identification of the bidders' signal distributions and complementarities. The model is estimated parametrically in three steps, and counterfactual analyses examine how bundling auctions affect revenue and efficiency. The results show that bidders gain positive complementarities from winning multiple auctions with similar characteristics. The counterfactual analysis shows that even though bundled auctions increase the platform revenue, they result in non-negligible allocative inefficiency.
